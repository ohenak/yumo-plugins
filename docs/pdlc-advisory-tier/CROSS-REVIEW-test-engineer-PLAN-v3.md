## Positive Observations

- **F-01 was resolved by stating a rule, not by patching two rows.** "A block's un-skipper is the task that lands the last symbol its cases exercise" is the general form of what I asked for, and the revision then *applies* it consistently across §3, §4, §4.1, §5.2, §8.1, §8.2 and §9.2 — including the non-obvious consequence that A-31, not A-30, owns the A1/A2 gate block because A2's `verifyGate` is the last symbol. A rule that survives its own corner cases is worth more than the two edits it replaced.
- **The manifest widening turned an argued invariant into a mechanical one, and the PLAN proved it rather than asserting it.** Giving each 🟢 task the test file it un-skips looked at first like it would weaken the single-writer discipline; instead it hands enforcement to `computeWaves`/`pathsCollide`. I re-ran it: **20 waves, member-for-member identical to `computeTopologicalBatches`' 20 batches**, i.e. nothing had to be split — no two un-skippers of one file can share a wave, and now the *code* says so rather than the prose. §4's citations for why overlap is legal (`:2334-2336`, `:2344-2367`, `:2377`) are all exact.
- **A-01's config pin now asserts a positive on both branches**, which is a better answer than the guard I asked for. The absent-file branch does not merely avoid failing — it pins the documented degradation (`parseImplementationConfig(null)` ⇒ defaults, `testCommand === null`, `orchestrate-dev.js:160-164`, `:188`), so the CI leg proves the legacy self-report path is reachable. Neither branch is vacuous and neither is a silent skip. That is exactly the shape a presence-conditional oracle should have.
- **T-02-4/T-02-5's relocation is right on the merits, not just on the schedule.** FSPEC:287-288 defines both over the driver's attempt loop; hosting them in `advisoryVerdict.test.js` was a mis-home independent of un-skip timing, and §8.1 now honestly reports that `advisoryVerdict.test.js` carries no FSPEC case at all — a count the revision could easily have fudged.
- **Q-03's answer keeps set-equality at one enumeration site.** Splitting the *block* rather than the *file*, generating the five per-seam cases from one in-file registry, and comparing that registry's key set to `ADVISORY_SEAMS` in A-22's block at batch 9 means a sixth seam fails before any per-seam block is un-skipped, and a deleted case is a deleted registry row. That is completeness by set-equality rather than containment, preserved through a refactor that could easily have lost it.
- **P-9's empty-input handling is the right instinct applied to itself.** Refusing to pin `governingClass([])`, recording *why* ("the test would assert the implementer's pick rather than the spec"), carrying it as an open item and raising an erratum — and then P-4 explicitly declining to constrain `matched` "because TSPEC declares its type and nothing more" — is the lesson generalised one row later. That is the difference between fixing a finding and absorbing it.
- **§6.4's exclusion of the two pre-existing queue functions is argued from the tool, not from convenience.** "The percentage cannot express 'part of a function'" is exactly right about a `fnMap`-name reducer, and naming the four behavioural cases that carry the evidence instead (T-04-1/2/3/3b) means the coverage floor stays mechanical without pretending to measure something it cannot.
- **The revision history is honest about supersession.** The 1.2 row is annotated in place ("**superseded in 1.3** — the runner commits once per task") rather than quietly rewritten. A reader of §10 can reconstruct why the red-evidence contract changed, which is the whole value of keeping the row.
