## Positive Observations

- **The correction to §7.2 was made by measuring, not by arguing.** v2's F-01 said the "one helper" claim was false; the revision did not defend it, it grepped both modules and published the result as a table with four verified sites, then made the resulting claim *assertable* rather than merely asserted. I re-ran the same measurement and C-a…C-d's four rows are exactly right, line for line. §14.4 records the method change too ("produced by grepping … rather than by reasoning about which helper *should* own them"), which is the durable part.
- **§6.2 is now the clearest section in the document, and it answers the criterion rather than the objection.** v2's F-03 asked for the resolvable case; the revision reframed the whole exemption around the right distinction — *never refuse* is not *never resolve* — states both branches, states the exit code for both, and then names the two assertions (a) and (b) so AC-1.4's two states cannot be half-tested. §11 gained a row for the second state rather than leaving §11 and §6.2 to disagree.
- **E-3's re-sourcing fixes the oracle and explains why.** The two-state table plus "an expected set that reads the artifact under test is not an expectation" is the general principle behind the specific fix, and pointing at PF-3 as the pattern already in the document makes it a consistency argument rather than a one-off patch. The "20 members before N-2 and 21 after" sentence is what makes the flip a visible edit.
- **The kind-4 scope erratum was reduced rather than re-asked.** §7.2 found that FSPEC BR-9.3 had already decided the harness/agent principle and downgraded the erratum to a wording confirmation, citing DEC-ERR-01 in §14.4 for why re-asking a settled question is the anti-pattern. The C-b consequence — marked commit message, unmarked file contents, no contradiction — is exactly the sentence that stops a future reviewer re-opening it, and §12.3 asserts the two halves separately.
- **§6.4 states a behaviour regression instead of hiding it in a branch.** "A repo with a corrupt `.claude/pdlc.config.json` and no `engine` section runs today with a notice (`run.mjs:184-192`) and refuses after this change" is verified at HEAD, is a real cost to a real operator, and is written as a trade with its price named. The three-row case table then forces the newly-refusing case into the totality tests rather than leaving the carve-out sentence to be misread.
- **§9.3's structural oracle is the right kind of pairing.** The container leg proves the refusal, the in-process parse proves the guard still runs first, and the document says plainly which regression each one cannot see. Keeping the `bin/pdlc.mjs` name so the manifest's `bin` field (`pdlc/engine/package.json:6-8`, verified), AC-2.1's `PATH` entry and the shipped `cli.test.js` target are all untouched turns a risky split into a no-op for every consumer-visible contract.
