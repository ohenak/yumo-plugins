**The headline learning.** A REQ that ran **nine dual-panel review rounds never converged, and the reason was growth, not disagreement.** Neither reviewer ever contested user need, scope, priority, phasing or any of the six decisions. Every round both panels closed every prior finding — SE closed 5/5, 3/3, 3/3 and TE closed 6/6 for six consecutive rounds with nothing carried — and every round both panels filed *new* findings, essentially all of them located in the text the previous revision had just added. Blocking counts by round: SE 10, 5, 5, 5, 5, 4, 3, 0, 3; TE 7, 4, 5, 4, 6, 2, 3, 1, 3. The document went from ~1,000 to **2,629 lines / 311 KB**. This is the REQ's own §1.2 P-1 (*new text is unreviewed text, so the finding rate is self-sustaining*) demonstrated on itself: **the review loop cannot converge on a document that grows faster than the panel can trace its boundary.** The fix was not another round; it was to stop and split the document.

## 4. Process Learnings

**4.1 The loop's failure mode here was document growth, not reviewer disagreement — and no mechanism in the pipeline observes growth.** Nine rounds, two reviewers, zero contested decisions, and a document that tripled in size. Both `MAX_REVIEW_ROUNDS` and any count-based fixed point are blind to this: they measure findings, and the finding rate was *stable* precisely because the surface being reviewed kept expanding. The operator's only effective lever was outside the loop entirely (stop, split, restart). **Candidate mechanism: a review loop should observe the size of the artifact it is converging on, and a document that grows past a threshold during its own review should be a halt with a "split this document" recommendation, not a further round.** This is the meta-lesson and the reason the four `pdlc-rcv-*` successors are each deliberately small and independently reviewable.

**4.2 A zero-delta round is real and was observed on this very feature.** (SE v3 F-08, TE v3 F-07, both `Process`.) Round 3 was dispatched over a byte-identical document — *the exact class of waste this REQ exists to bound, observed on the REQ that specifies it*. The loop compared blocking counts (trivially equal) but never the document bytes. Mechanised as AC-2.8 + `DOC-SHA256:`; the mechanism is carried by `pdlc-rcv-fixed-point-stop` (REQ-RCV-02) and **should be treated as a prerequisite for any other convergence work**, because until it ships every other measurement can be taken over an unchanged document.

**4.3 The POSTMORTEM → `RESOLVED: yes` → second-window path worked exactly as designed, and it is the reason nine rounds were possible.** `MAX_REVIEW_ROUNDS` halted at 5; the operator landed the round-5 findings as v1.4, verified the revision-note claims *against the document body rather than against the note*, and cleared the marker. The mechanism did its job. The learning is that **clearing a POSTMORTEM re-arms a loop whose underlying failure mode was never addressed** — the second window reproduced the first, one altitude down. Clearing should be paired with a stated hypothesis about what will be different, and the POSTMORTEM's own recommendation ("if round 6 again returns only new one-clause findings in new text, prefer carrying editorial-scale residue into FSPEC over opening round 7") was correct and was not followed.

**4.4 Reviewers under-tagged `Cross-Feature`, and self-corrected only after the third recurrence.** TE explicitly tagged the boundary-value defect `Cross-Feature` at v7 *"because the recurrence is why I tag it"* — the first two instances (v5 F-06, v6 F-02) were filed `Local`. Likewise TE v9 F-04 was filed as a mechanical fix twice before being promoted to a `Process` finding on its third occurrence in the third consecutive section. **A defect filed identically three times is a process signal by definition; reviewers should promote on the second recurrence, not the third.** This harvest re-routed several `Local`-tagged findings into §2 accordingly.

**4.5 Both panels ran a self-applied stopping rule and both reported it honestly against their own interest.** SE recorded that AC-2.1 *"would have halted this phase at round 7, one round before the approval it is now getting"*; TE recorded a rise at round 9 *"rather than soften it"*. The trajectory note is a cheap, high-value review artifact and should be standard: it produced the two best pieces of calibration evidence in the whole corpus.

**4.6 High-quality reviews are not free.** Eighteen cross-review files totalling ~530 KB — several individual reviews larger than most REQs. The reviews were excellent (every one traced claims to shipped code at named commits rather than taking the document's word), and that quality is exactly what made each round produce another generation of findings. **Review depth and document size compound; capping one without capping the other does not converge.**

## 5. Open Items for Consolidation

1. **Promote the growth-halt mechanism.** §4.1 is the central finding of this feature and has no home yet: no successor REQ owns "the loop observes artifact size". Candidate: a new `docs/_decisions/` entry, or a fifth `pdlc-rcv-*` REQ. Until then it lives only here.
2. **Strengthen DC-01 in `docs/_constraints/DOMAIN-CONSTRAINTS.md`** so a boundary string is not considered declared until its receive side is a total function stated in the *same* revision (§2, three-round recurrence).
3. **Record the "states vs strings" convergence heuristic** (§2, SE v8) as a stopping signal for reviewers — candidate for the `se-review` / `te-review` SKILL.md prompts, which currently offer only count-based trajectory guidance.
4. **Add a repair-safety constraint** (§2, SE v8 G-24): a per-reason repair table must state its effect on every counter it mutates, across every reachable value.
5. **Amend the review-round tagging guidance**: promote a recurring finding to `Cross-Feature`/`Process` on its **second** occurrence (§4.4).
6. **Record the supersession pattern itself.** Splitting a non-converging REQ into phased successors **with requirement ids unchanged**, plus a read-only shared baseline document for the analysis sections, worked cleanly and is reusable. Candidate: `docs/_decisions/`.
7. **Calibration follow-up.** `docs/pdlc-review-convergence-calibration/` was a deferral stub bound to this feature and is archived with it; its four open questions now belong to `docs/pdlc-rcv-fixed-point-stop`. Confirm nothing was lost in that hand-off.
