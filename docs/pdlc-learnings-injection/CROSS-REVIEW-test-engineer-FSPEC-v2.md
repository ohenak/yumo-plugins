| F-01 | High | Local | **E-05 and AT-28 keep a truncation branch that BR-3's own argument has just declared undecidable — the AT cannot be written except by echoing the implementation.** BR-3 removes `RSN-TRUNCATED` on the reasoning that "no predicate over a document's own bytes separates truncation from a document that legitimately lacks later sections, which E-19 makes eligible rather than defective" (FSPEC:289-291). E-19 is retained and says exactly that: a document missing priority sections is injected as far as it goes and is **not** unparseable (FSPEC:657). Yet E-05 (FSPEC:638) and AT-28 (FSPEC:821) require a fixture "whose bytes stop mid-document" to come out `RSN-UNPARSEABLE`. Those cannot both hold: a document truncated after its first section is byte-wise an E-19 document (eligible), and one truncated before any priority section is byte-wise an E-33 document (`RSN-NO-MATERIAL`). A test writer given AT-28 has no rule fixing which of the three outcomes their truncated fixture should produce, so the only way to make it green is to cut the fixture wherever the shipped predicate happens to reject — an expected value read off the code under test. Fix, either way: (a) delete E-05 and AT-28's truncation conjunct, since E-19 and E-33 already partition the observable space and BR-3 has ruled the third branch unconstructible; or (b) state in BR-3 the observable that makes bytes-stopping unparseable — e.g. "bytes stop before the document presents the shape the predicate requires" — and give AT-28 a **pair** of fixtures, one cut before that point asserted `RSN-UNPARSEABLE` and one cut after it asserted eligible under E-19, so the boundary is falsifiable rather than assumed. | BR-3, E-05, E-19, E-33, AT-28, D-6 |

## Positive Observations

- **BR-2's re-basing on the shipped enumeration is correct against HEAD, and corrects my own v1 assumption.** `pdlc/workflows/consolidate-learnings.js:1344-1345` pins exactly `:(glob)docs/*/LEARNINGS-*.md` and `:(glob)docs/completed/*/LEARNINGS-*.md`, with **no** `docs/discarded/` exclusion clause — the same pair the nudge hook carries (`pdlc/hooks/scripts/nudge-consolidation.sh:60`). Discarded documents live one level deeper (`docs/discarded/pdlc-rcv-budget-stop/LEARNINGS-pdlc-rcv-budget-stop.md`, `docs/discarded/pdlc-review-convergence/LEARNINGS-pdlc-review-convergence.md`), unmatched by either glob, and no LEARNINGS file sits directly at `docs/discarded/`. So AC-2.6 is satisfied by the enumeration's shape, exactly as BR-2 now says, and adding an explicit exclusion rule would indeed have changed the answer for paths the shipped predicate matches. This is the rare revision that made a rule *smaller* and more grounded at the same time.
- **`RSN-NO-MATERIAL` is a real branch, not a defensive one, and the cited fixture reproduces exactly.** `regime-ledger`'s `docs/completed/34-postgres-audit-repository/LEARNINGS-postgres-audit-repository.md` carries `## Implementation Learnings`, `## Cross-Feature Findings`, `## Process Findings` and nothing else — none of BR-6's five priority names, verbatim as claimed. Re-derived over both repositories, three documents carry no priority section under strict matching, so E-33 has fixture material that occurs naturally. Introducing the id also closes the zero-byte-contribution-consuming-a-slot hole that BR-8's rows could not otherwise distinguish from a real contribution.
- **BR-6's section-name citation checks out at HEAD.** `pdlc/skills/harvest-learnings/SKILL.md:87,103,110` carry `## 2. Cross-Feature Patterns`, `## 5. Open Items for Consolidation` and `## 6. Approval Record` under the numeric-prefix convention BR-6 describes, and routing which heading forms count as which section to F-O-1 rather than fixing literal strings here is the right altitude call.
- **AT-07's second regime is a genuinely good catch on the revision's own initiative.** Asserting the count bound under a corpus where `maxTotalBytes` binds first — contributing count strictly below `maxDocuments`, with `RSN-BYTES` rows — prevents the count-bound test from passing only in the toy regime where fixtures are small enough for it to bind. Paired with BR-5's measured admission that the total bound is the one that binds on real corpora, this is the sort of test that survives contact with production.
- **AT-14's two-process requirement and AT-34's shared-instrument requirement both landed as stated**, and neither was weakened in the process — the disabled-run claim now rides on a control that is proven to fire.
