## Positive Observations

- **The four blocking findings were addressed at the locus, not papered over.** Each of F-01…F-04 produced a structural change — a named record (`ruleInputs.thresholds`), a split green with its own §Traceability row, a new red-before-green edge (`LI-19 → LI-07`), and a whole new gate wording with a ledger — rather than a sentence conceding the point. The AT-15 fix in particular does the harder thing: it keeps the test whole in one suite so §T.5's partition and `LI-T-SUITEMAP`'s disjointness are untouched, and pays for that by carrying the test in the expected-red ledger for three batches. That is the right trade for a PM: FSPEC AT-15's three clauses stay one acceptance test, which is how the REQ AC-2.6 promise reads to an operator.
- **The expected-red ledger is a genuinely better artifact than the gate it replaces.** It is stated in test names wherever a suite is split, it shrinks by exactly what each batch claims to green, and it makes a *premature* green a signal rather than a bonus ("a suite dropping out of the ledger early is as much a failure as one lingering"). That third property is the one I would not have thought to ask for, and it is what stops a task quietly greening something it never claimed and hiding a mis-scoped implementation.
- **The new coverage measurement reproduces digit-for-digit, and its failure mode is stated honestly.** I re-ran the exact two-stage command §Verification now prints, on a committed tree: stage 1 gives `Test Suites: 98 passed, 98 total` / `Tests: 70 skipped, 3828 passed, 3898 total`, exit 0; stage 2 gives `orchestrate-dev.js` 97.27 / **88.14** / 95.02 / 97.27, `build-runtime.mjs` 88.23 branches, `orchestrate-queue.js` 88.75 branches, exit 0 — every figure identical to the document's. The admission that the bare `npm run test:coverage` **cannot** be the gate (stage 1 inherits the two `documentOracles` failures and exits before stage 2) is the kind of measured negative result plans usually omit, and DoD 11's "3.14 points of headroom" plus H-8 turn it into a number someone can be held to.
- **LI-23 converts a human reading into an oracle, in the right shape.** The §T.7 fail-open inventory was a table walked by a person; it is now three set equalities against the frozen catalogues `LEARNINGS_CORPUS_OUTCOMES`, `LEARNINGS_REJECT_REASONS`, `LEARNINGS_NOTICES` — set equality, never containment, "so an arm that silently stops being entered **and** an invented code both red". That is exactly the completeness-by-set-equality bar, and the paired positive/negative form means the absence claim has a positive companion on the same instrument. LI-22's row-by-row walk correctly demotes itself to a cross-check that the table and the suite name the same twelve arms.
- **LI-06's three-step mutation proof falsifies the one oracle with no red predecessor.** Flip a byte ⇒ the digest assertion reds; delete a `{caseId}` ⇒ the **set-equality** conjunct reds ("the conjunct containment would have let pass"); add a spurious `{caseId}` ⇒ set equality reds. Each step targets a different clause, all three are required, and "a step that does not red is a halt, not a pass". A guard authored green over its own artifact is the classic vacuous-green shape, and this is the discipline that makes it non-vacuous.
- **LI-02's "no jest globals in this helper" is a small rule with a real product consequence.** The capture script (LI-05) imports the fixture matrix from a plain node process; a `jest.fn` in the helper would break the baseline capture, and the baseline is what every byte-identity promise in DoD 4 is measured against. Naming it in the row rather than discovering it at batch 3 protects AC-4.1/AC-5.1a directly.
- **The file-ownership manifest now parses, and its arithmetic is checkable.** One row per (file, owning task), files with no owning task moved to prose, and the reconciliation restated: 24 rows over 17 distinct files, "every one of the 23 tasks in §Batches owns at least one row". The `package.json` non-ownership is now argued (the `c8` block resolves relative to `pdlc/workflows/`, so a root-level script cannot be included without re-rooting it) rather than silent, with named oracles standing in for the missing coverage floor.

## Recommendation

**Needs revision**

One High finding is open, and it is new to v0.2 rather than inherited: **F-07** — LI-01's `learningsPremises.test.js` asserts the §Overview change-surface table's **absence** claims while promising to stay green, and LI-04, LI-05 and LI-06 are scheduled to falsify those very claims at batches 3 and 4. Under §Verification's batch gates and P-A-2's halt rule, the PLAN as written halts its own wave at batch 3.

Exactly what must change:

1. Scope `learningsPremises.test.js` to the existence-and-shape premises only — P-1, P-2a, P-3, P-4, P-7/P-8, P-10 — and remove "and the §Overview change-surface table's absence claims" from LI-01's assertion list, which also resolves the row's internal contradiction with its own "**Asserts existence and shape only**".
2. Keep the absence checks as LI-01's **one-time pre-flight**, recorded in the completion note alongside the H-2 engine-failure triage that already lives there. They are a batch-1 measurement, not a standing invariant.
3. If any absence claim is instead kept as a standing test, list it in the expected-red ledger for the batch that falsifies it and restate the ledger's "shrinks monotonically to empty" property accordingly — but this route costs the ledger its cleanest property, so route 2 is preferred.

F-08 (DoD 4's broken sentence join) is Low and non-gating; fix it in the same pass. Everything else in the v0.1→v0.2 delta lands, and my four prior High findings are closed at the locus rather than argued away. Nothing in the revision broke a section I approved in v1: the §T.5 partition still sums to 35, the manifest arithmetic reconciles, and the coverage baseline reproduces exactly.
