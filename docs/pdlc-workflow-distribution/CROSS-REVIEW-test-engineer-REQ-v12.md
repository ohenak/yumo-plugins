## Positive Observations

- **The reachability derivation in AC-2.9(2a) is the right kind of repair.** It does not assert that my finding was accepted; it re-runs the three experiments, prints their return codes, states the generalisation, and then records that v11's own mandated test would have been *red against a correct implementation*. A REQ that documents the falsity of its previous revision's fixture is doing the work that stops the same fixture being re-derived at FSPEC.
- **Moving the ordering oracle to a script-owned trace, and saying why a `strace`/`dtruss` tracer and a `mkdir` shim are each insufficient, settles the layer question permanently.** The rejected-alternatives paragraph in AC-2.9(4) is worth more than the decision itself: it tells the FSPEC author which options are already closed and on what evidence. My F-01 is about the assertion's shape, not about this choice, which is correct.
- **Test (e) is exactly the overlap fixture the top-of-precedence claim needed, and its choice of `manifest-malformed` is justified from reachability** ("it reaches the writer, unlike `repo-root-unresolved`, which has no write target at all") rather than picked arbitrarily. That reasoning generalises to any future precedence member.
- **Renumbering `readBytes_json` to `10`/`11`/`12` is a cheaper fix than the conjunct I proposed, and the REQ says so with the measurement.** Recording that the collision would have false-greened the one exit that is *also* skipped at uid 0 — invisible on both runners — is the kind of second-order reasoning that makes a coverage argument trustworthy.
- **Scoping the 85% branch floor to a non-root runner, and naming the four invariants a root run does not verify, converts a silent coverage hole into a declared one.** Tying it to D-DIST-06 rather than to "we will notice when the number drops" is the correct place for that dependency.
- **Conditioning AC-1.8(iv)'s axis 2 on axis 5 and stating the sub-product's cardinality (48 + 24, never 96)** makes the totality property mechanically checkable by counting, which is the only way a reviewer can verify a generator claim without running it.
