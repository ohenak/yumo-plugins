**Scope of this review.** Testability only. REQ v17.0 is approved at product scope and its §10
TSPEC/PROPERTIES rows are downstream by design — no finding below asks the FSPEC to do TSPEC's or
PROPERTIES' work. Every finding is either (a) an internal contradiction that makes a stated oracle

self-contradictory, or (c) an acceptance test in §12 whose fixture is unconstructible or whose
oracle cannot fail.

(consistent with §10 O-13). No standing constraint or promoted decision was available to check

## Positive Observations

- §5.6's restore oracle ("a hash comparison can pass against a backup that was never written; a restore cannot") is exactly the right instinct — it replaces a false-greenable oracle with one that traverses the real recovery path.
- §5.8's post-run observation point, the four worked rows, and the derived "exit 1 is reachable only under `--check`" with the explicit instruction that no test should attempt to construct it, is a clean and complete disposition of O-14. Deriving an *unreachability* result and telling TSPEC not to build the fixture is the correct altitude.
- §4.4's rung-(ii) cause→`unlink`→rung table (O-5) does the same job for fixture economy, and §7.4's `--porcelain`-not-`git diff` reasoning correctly identifies that the landing commit's `dist/` is entirely untracked — a genuine false-green that the earlier form would have shipped.
- §3.6's refusal to import v13's axis tables (24 of 96 cells undefined) and its naming of the two concrete determinism hazards (directory order, locale/environment order) gives PROPERTIES a real starting point without pretending to do its work.
- §6.2's mapping is specified as a pure function of a parsed record, explicitly unit-testable against literals with no filesystem — the right test level for that component.
- §13.1's structural discharge of NFR-2, with the explicit prohibition on wall-clock assertions and a release-checklist observation instead, avoids a flaky-by-construction timing test.
