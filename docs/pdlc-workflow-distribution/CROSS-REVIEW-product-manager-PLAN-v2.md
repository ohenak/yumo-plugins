## Positive Observations

- **PO-1 — the F-01 fix is stronger than the fix I asked for, and the two halves are independent.** I asked for a resequence **or** a compensating control; v2.0 ships both, and they fail independently. If an implementer commits a landing batch against instruction (per-batch commits are, as §1 itself notes, the dispatcher default for batches 1–13), the resequencing still means the committed state is fully functional: `.worktreeinclude`, execute bits, the hook registration, the bootstrap docs and the document sweep are all in before anything destructive, and only L-01 (batch 20) and later would need reverting. Conversely, if the order were disturbed, defer-commit still keeps `HEAD` at the batch-13 state. And §5's "halt state must match `git status`" is a mechanical detector of the stray commit — a committed batch shows a clean tree. That is defence in depth, not a restated intention. (F-02 above is a wording defect in that detector, not in the design.)
- **PO-2 — the one-commit contradiction is genuinely gone.** §8's "one commit containing all of §4.1's landing rows" is now derivable from §1's rule, repeated in all eight L-rows, gated in §5, and executed by L-09. There is no reading of the document in which both checks cannot hold.
- **PO-3 — REQ-DIST-05 is now traceable end to end.** T-14 names `artifactVersion` and `pluginVersion` with the `plugin.json` source, §9 carries a REQ-DIST-05 line, and AC-5.3 — a **P2** — is scoped to R-12 and routed in §7 rather than silently absorbed. A P1 restored and a P2 explicitly deferred with an owner is the correct product disposition, not a narrowing.
- **PO-4 — the pending-REQ decision is product-sound and correctly reasoned.** "Editing an approved, queued REQ from inside a different feature's landing commit would silently amend another feature's approved upstream artifact, and no reviewer of *those* REQs would see it" is the right product call — the alternative buys drift-hygiene with an unreviewed cross-feature edit. It is also correctly typed as a **queue-time** risk rather than a landing-time one. Only the placement of the mitigation is wrong (F-01); the decision itself should stand.
- **PO-5 — §6.3 closes a blind spot rather than describing it.** Each of D-1/D-2/D-3 carries an oracle expressed as an observable property of the file's final text ("the section names `pdlc/workflows/dist/` **and** `distribution-manifest.json`, and contains no line asserting that `build-runtime.mjs` writes into `.claude/workflows/`") that deliberately does **not** route through `coveredViolations`, which is blind to it by construction. That is the correct shape for the half of REQ §6's bullet that has no natural oracle.
- **PO-6 — PL-05 is the right way to handle an upstream tension at PLAN altitude.** AT-7's claim bundles a classification fact, an entrypoint fact and a queue fact; the PLAN splits the *execution* across batches 8/11/12, keeps §14's file placement exactly, records the straddle, and proposes **no** TSPEC edit. No acceptance criterion is narrowed — both conjuncts are asserted by batch 11 — and the approved upstream is left alone.
- **PO-7 — §3 note 10 states the property-gate trade-off in the terms a product owner needs**: what it costs (a vacuous property halts at 100% built / 0% landed), why it is taken, what v2.0's resequencing bought (the halt now lands at a fully functional `HEAD`), and the manual fallback if the operator lands anyway. That is a decision, not a rationalisation.
