## Positive Observations

- The three vacuity arguments in §3.5, §5.1 and §7.1 ("a trap that never fires is indistinguishable
  from one that was never installed") are exactly the right instinct, and §7.3's insistence that the
  parity double must **not** write the approval anchors — because the anchors are the module's own
  `_appendFile` write at `orchestrate-dev.js:6190` — is the sharpest anti-vacuity clause I have read
  in this feature's documents. F-03 is a mechanism gap, not a disagreement with the goal.
- §7.3's negative half ("a write-less double must *fail* this test", AT-ENG-45) is the falsifying
  test the oracle needs, stated before the oracle. §6.3 (F-04) should be given the same treatment.
- The HEAD-state grounding is unusually careful and almost entirely accurate: 60+ `file:line`
  citations checked, three off by 1–3 lines (F-15) and none wrong about behaviour. §1.3's four
  structural changes, §2.4's two anti-fork observables and §5.1's account of `classifyThrown`
  (`transport.mjs:98`, `:123` funnelling the unrecognised to `TransportError`) all verify against
  source. The `_git` identity argument (`branchGuardTransport`, `orchestrate-dev.js:3487`, requires
  `_git !== defaultGit`) is a genuine, non-obvious hazard caught with the right remedy — a test on
  the inequality.
- §3.1's seam table is the exhaustive answer G-2 was owed, and the `_runCommand` row correctly
  identifies the silent-degradation hazard (a null seam drops Phase I to the legacy self-report
  gate) rather than listing the seam neutrally. Leaving `_sessionAgent` deliberately unpainted (O-6)
  is the right call for a seam whose absence is today's semantics.
- §4.5's "counts are present-and-zero, never absent" carries the advisory tier's hard-won
  distinction into the engine block, and it is the property that makes a quiet run and a broken
  counter distinguishable in a test.

## Recommendation

**Needs revision**

Seven High findings. They cluster in two places, and neither is a disagreement with the design's
intent:

1. **The suite's central mechanism does not exist yet.** F-03 is the load-bearing one — all three
   set-equality harnesses assume shared in-process state that `node --test` does not provide, and
   the outcome harness fails *green*. F-11 is the same root cause for the hermeticity bootstrap.
   Both are fixable with one decision about how the suite is invoked, made once in §7.
2. **Four oracles cannot yet be written from the text**: the guard-parity assertions (F-04), the
   `agent-reported-failure` predicate (F-05), the skill-set derivation scan (F-07), and AC-1.2's
   filesystem observation (F-08). Each needs a named mechanism, not more prose.

F-01 and F-06 are contradictions to resolve rather than work to schedule, and both are one edit each.
The Medium findings are concrete and small. The design itself — the layering, the seam contract, the
transport interface, the report block — reads as implementable, and I expect one revision round to
clear this.
