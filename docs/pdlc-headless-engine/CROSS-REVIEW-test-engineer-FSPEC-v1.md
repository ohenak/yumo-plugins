## Positive Observations

- §8.2's eight-sequence table is internally consistent under both budgets — I re-derived every row against "3 retries after the first attempt" plus the per-dispatch one-timeout cap and each terminal classification and attempt count holds, including the two rows that distinguish cap-terminal from budget-terminal. That table is directly transcribable into a parameterised test, which is exactly what AT-ENG-35 asks for.
- §5.1's six rows are genuinely total and genuinely first-match: I checked the uncovered state (no token, no key, no settings evidence) lands on row 6, and BR-AUTH-1 states the overlap resolution explicitly. AC-2.4's paired positive in §5.4 is the right shape — banner id, a *completing* dispatch, and a recorded source per dispatch, rather than asserting the key's absence.
- BR-MODEL-2/3 is the strongest oracle in the document: a set-equality in both directions over recorded *descriptors*, with M-ENG-07 supplying the provocation that reaches every row and EC-DISP-6 stating that an unreachable row is fixed by extending the corpus, never by loosening the oracle. F-05 asks only that §8.1 and §12.3 be given the same treatment.
- §17.3's "every unhandled case falls one way — refuse, report, cost nothing further" gives a reviewer a single falsifiable claim to test the document against, and I could not find a case in §3–§12 that falls the other way.
- The AT numbering is contiguous and complete (AT-ENG-01…66 across §3.5–§12.6, no gaps, no duplicates), §14.1 carries a row for all 26 REQ acceptance criteria, and §18.2 correctly promotes the three assertions that quantify over the whole run rather than sampling — AT-ENG-X1 in particular is the "per-section tests sample, this one quantifies" discipline done right.
- §13.1 raises three upstream defects as errata rather than silently deciding them, and records the interim behaviour each section assumed — a reviewer can see the exposure instead of reconstructing it.

## Recommendation

**Needs revision**

Five High findings. Three of them (F-02, F-03, F-05) make named acceptance tests underivable as written — a blocked run's exit code, the fixture for half of the billing-posture table, and the reverse direction of two set-equalities §18.3 requires the suite to pin. F-01 leaves a closed-set assertion contradicted by the shipped CLI with no red/green story, and F-04 leaves the delete guard — this document's own "largest open safety gap" — provable by a test that need not run under the permission posture a real dispatch carries. None of the five requires new behaviour: each is a matter of naming an observable, a corpus, or an ordering that the document already has the vocabulary for (§7.3's corpus device and §12.2's fixed-fixture device are both good enough to reuse).
