## Positive Observations

- **The document does the job a DECISIONS artifact exists for.** Every entry states what was decided, what was rejected, what constraint forced the shape, how reversible it is, and what would make it worth revisiting — and §0 draws the boundary explicitly (open questions stay open; mechanism with no live alternative stays in TSPEC). That is the discipline that stops the next reader re-opening settled ground.
- **The rejected alternatives carry costs, not just verdicts.** §7's right-hand column ("what the rejection costs") is the part these documents usually omit, and the framing at `:642` — "a rejected alternative with no cost is not a decision worth recording" — is the right standard. §8 then converts those costs into PLAN obligations and standing costs, which is exactly the hand-off PLAN and PROPERTIES need.
- **DEC-ENG-03 surfaces the guard's fail-open branch instead of inheriting it.** Finding a shipped script's fail-open interpreter probe, naming it as a consequence the decision *accepts responsibility for*, refusing to rely on it, and declining to edit the plugin path's interactive behaviour (NG-1) is careful product reasoning. F-01/F-02 are about where the remedy is authorised, not about the judgement.
- **The vacuous-green failure mode is treated as a first-class product risk.** DEC-ENG-04, DEC-ENG-10 and DEC-ENG-11 each reject the cheaper mechanism specifically because it produces a green suite that proves nothing, and DEC-ENG-10's analysis of *asymmetric* vacuity (catalogue direction fails loudly, outcome direction passes over the empty set, `:450-452`) is the kind of reasoning that protects users from a pipeline that reports success it did not earn.
- **Decisions that are refusals to build are recorded as decisions.** DEC-ENG-14's "Constraint that forced this shape: none — which is exactly the point" (`:625`) is the honest form: the gap is left open deliberately and recorded so it is not later read as an oversight. Same for DEC-ENG-05's note that deleting `EXPECTED_SKILLS` loses an assertion, recorded so the reduction is not mistaken for a mistake.
- **Reversibility is assessed per decision and not uniformly optimistic.** DEC-ENG-04's "easy per branch, hard in spirit" and DEC-ENG-13's "easy structurally, harder socially" are the two entries where a blanket "easy" would have been misleading, and both are flagged — including in the §9 index, which carries the qualification rather than flattening it.

## Recommendation

**Needs revision** — two High findings, both about the same seam: DEC-ENG-03 originates an operator-facing precondition and refusal that no REQ constraint or FSPEC rule authorises, and places it on an EC row whose message contract cannot honestly describe it.

To close, in order:

1. **F-01** — take the interpreter precondition upstream. Emit a REQ constraint (a working Python interpreter is required on any host running the engine unattended) and an FSPEC EC row for the refusal; then rewrite DEC-ENG-03's "The decision, given that" paragraph to *cite* that authority instead of establishing it. The engineering judgement stands as written; only its home changes.
2. **F-02** — stop borrowing EC-GUARD-4's message contract. Give the interpreter refusal its own obligations (name the missing interpreter and the three candidates probed, name the remedy) and scope `AT-ENG-43`'s three-obligation assertion to the transport-capability case, since naming `transport-cli.mjs` as the known alternative is false on a host with no interpreter.
3. **F-03** — restore TSPEC's hedge in §8's obligation row, or number the probe as rung 6 with an FSPEC ladder row so BR-START-2's totality can report it. Either way, state whether dry-run non-fatality is intended for this check.
4. **F-04** — re-cite: BR-GUARD-5 / O-2 carry the measure-first obligation; BR-GUARD-4 is the gap statement.
5. **F-05** — pin FSPEC v1.5 in the Upstream row and confirm no decision rests on superseded v1.3 text.
6. **F-06** — correct the derived scanner set to `{ship-pr, dod-verify, se-implement}` and the PHASE_DISPATCH-only role count to four, in both DEC-ENG-05 and §7's row.

F-03 through F-06 are Medium and non-gating on their own; they are listed because they are all one-line edits and cheap to land in the same pass as the two Highs. Nothing in this review asks for a decision to be reversed — the decisions themselves are well-argued, and every code claim I could check held except the six corrections above.

One erratum is raised against TSPEC rather than folded into this verdict (see the response trailer): §6.1's enumeration of the guard's decision procedure begins at the JSON parse and does not carry the interpreter probe branch, which is the upstream gap DEC-ENG-03 correctly noticed at `:157-159`.
