Engineering memo · pdlc · 2026-08-27

PDLC Pipeline Optimization

Speed and token economics without losing the gates.

Author: analysis pass over two corpora Audience: Kane Ho, pipeline author/operator Status: proposal — nothing shipped

§0Executive summary

The pdlc pipeline converges on correctness and it converges expensively. Across two independent repositories — yumo-plugins (6 un-consolidated LEARNINGS to the 2026-08-19 boundary) and regime-ledger (8 features surveyed) — the same signature appears: a document reaches substantive stability in roughly rounds 1–3, then spends rounds 4–15 re-proving that nothing changed. pdlc-engineering-loop produced 172 cross-review files with 4 of 6 documents pinned at the 15-round lifetime cap and 114 approving verdicts recorded on the way to that ceiling; regime-swing-confirmation produced 188 cross-review files with five documents at the same cap; longhorizon-product-scaffold produced 112 with DECISIONS v2–v7 carrying one identical approval hash six rounds running. The waste is not review rigor — it is the absence of a mechanism that can say "settled". The moves below fall into three tiers: engine defects that can be fixed today with no process risk, config-gated loop-economics rules that change when a round is allowed to exist, and a process redesign that front-loads discovery into a bounded grilling phase so that FSPEC review starts from decisions already made rather than discovering them across thirteen rounds.

§1Where the cost lives

The dispatch model

A phase that passes on round 1 costs 2 dispatches (author, reviewer). Best case end-to-end, before Phase I fan-out, is 22–24 dispatches: R 2, F 3, T+D 3–7, P 3, PR 3, PT 1, CR 2, DOD 1, H 1, PUB 1–2, MERGE 0. Each additional review round costs +2–3. Each erratum batch costs +3–4 (route, targeted edit, delta-confirmation, and — when the bounded follow-up fires — a re-confirmation).

The budget structure is the multiplier: MAX_REVIEW_ROUNDS = 5 is per invocation, while MAX_LIFETIME_ROUNDS = 15 is per document type. A document that keeps being re-opened — staled approval anchor, erratum cascade, forced re-run — accumulates rounds without bound until the lifetime cap absorbs it, at which point the document is accepted as-is: not an approval, not a failure (pdlc/OPERATIONS.md, §Review loop mechanics).

Headline finding

Two independent repositories, no shared features, no shared authors of the specs — and the same signature. Most late rounds re-prove that nothing changed. On longhorizon-product-scaffold, DECISIONS v2–v7 carried the identical approval hash 3e282f13… across six consecutive rounds and PLAN v2–v7 carried 693805d4…. On pdlc-engineering-loop, one stale dispatch hash was re-filed as a finding in 54 separate reviews with zero edits owed. The loop cannot distinguish "still working" from "nothing left to say, and nobody told the driver". This cross-repo recurrence was recorded as this repo's first cross-repo consolidation promotions (consolidation pass 2026-08-27-2): DEC-ERRROUTE-04 and DEC-ANCHOR-01.

Prompt payload anatomy

Per-dispatch cost is dominated by three things, in order: the resume opener, which embeds the entire document under review; the learnings suffix, budgeted at ≤20 KB (5 documents × 6 KB); and the fixed clause boilerplate re-sent every round — ORACLE_QUALITY_CLAUSE, ERRATUM_PROTOCOL_CLAUSE, PACING_CONTRACT_CLAUSE, plus the per-docType grounding clause. Reviewer and author SKILL.md files run 279–331 lines of which roughly 45–55% is shared procedural boilerplate (Git Workflow, Review Process, Delta Re-Review, verdict contract); the role judgment is concentrated in a single "Review Scope" block per file. Prompt-content redundancy has largely already been addressed — REVIEW_CONVERGENCE_CLAUSE makes rounds 2+ delta-scoped with a git-diff instruction and a High-only bar. The remaining redundancy is structural, not textual: too many rounds, not too many tokens per round.

§2Diagnosis: six failure modes

D1 — Cascade and pin re-confirmation dominance

An upstream document moves; every downstream document's pins, hashes, section anchors and line references drift; the loop responds by opening a full review round on each downstream document. On macro-nightly-job, rounds 3 and beyond were overwhelmingly cascade-confirmation. On longhorizon-daily-baseline, roughly half of TSPEC's 12 rounds were delta re-confirmations — about 15 re-confirmation dispatches plus 4 erratum re-grounds. On iv-snapshot-store-postgres, PLAN v4–v7 and v10–v15 plus TSPEC v7–v9 were entirely provenance bookkeeping — SHAs, counts, line numbers — and a single squash invalidated 17 in-prose SHAs at once. The harvest note from that corpus states it plainly: the expensive part is not getting the spec right, it is proving repeatedly that upstream edits did not silently invalidate approved downstream text. This pattern recurred independently in yumo-plugins's own pdlc-wave-resume feature, and the two corpora's agreement is now named as a decision: docs/_decisions/DECISIONS-erratum-routing.md (DEC-ERRROUTE-04, cross-repo consolidation pass 2026-08-27-2).

D2 — The cap does the converging

Documents do not stop because a reviewer says "converged"; they stop because they hit 15. pdlc-engineering-loop: 4 of 6 documents at the cap, 114 approving verdicts recorded. regime-swing-confirmation: REQ, FSPEC, TSPEC, PLAN and PROPERTIES all at 15, DECISIONS at 14. paper-book-strategy-generalization: TSPEC, PLAN and PROPERTIES at 15, DECISIONS at 14 — and the harvest notes the loop was converging on the 5-round per-invocation budget while the lifetime budget kept re-opening it. The verdict-at-cap terminal state is now named as a defect in docs/_decisions/DECISIONS-loop-termination.md (DEC-TERM-01).

D3 — The erratum channel routes but never closes

Three failures compound here. First, routing without minting: a finding recorded in a confirmation cross-review that mints no erratum item is a routing defect, not reviewer discretion (DEC-ERRROUTE-01) — pdlc-wave-resume's own POSTMORTEM concludes the routing failure "will recur"; pdlc-learnings-injection had 3 erratum halts on this channel, the first two the same failure two phases apart. Second, channel under-sizing: a fixed 1–2 round budget fed a full architectural section is a mis-sized channel (DEC-ERRROUTE-02) — pdlc-engineering-loop halted Phase P on this. Third, partial re-grounding, which DEC-ERRROUTE-03 rates as worse than none: four independent instances of a dispatch absorbing only the named items while other changes in the same upstream interval went silently unabsorbed, leaving a document that reads as reconciled and is not. On pdlc-engineering-loop a truncate/restore occurred inside a confirmed delta — the round confirmed a state its own edit then reverted.

D4 — Staleness bookkeeping re-filed as findings

The single most mechanical waste in the corpus. One stale dispatch hash re-filed as a Low finding in 54 reviews on pdlc-engineering-loop, with zero edits owed. Eight findings in one pdlc-wave-resume erratum round were hand-copied pins that are hash-derivable. On structure-directional-options-scoring, an approval anchor was re-filed across four separate reviews, and rounds 10+ were stale-citation churn (159 FINDING: lines total, 124 of them cross-feature-tagged). DEC-TERM-02 now states that a round whose only delta is staleness bookkeeping is not a review round.

D5 — Uniform six-document ceremony regardless of feature size

Every feature gets REQ, FSPEC, TSPEC, PLAN, PROPERTIES and (when warranted) DECISIONS, each with its own review window and its own lifetime cap. pdlc-rcv-budget-stop produced a 61 KB REQ at 99.8% of the size budget for one constant and roughly four pure functions. pdlc-review-convergence grew its REQ from 1,000 to 2,629 lines / 311 KB before being discarded at round 9. On regime-swing-confirmation the REQ hit 92% of the 61,440-byte ceiling by round 2, and ~90% of it was ultimately relocated to docs/_constraints/ — meaning the reviewers spent rounds on material that did not belong in the REQ at all.

D6 — Post-implementation triple coverage

The shipped diff is evaluated three times through fully separate chains: Phase CR (pm/te lens cross-review), Phase DOD (dod-verify's stub / mock-data / unwired-integration / coverage scan), and the PROPERTIES V-wave. CR and DOD have adjacent-but-not-identical lenses and no shared state; the operator decision of 2026-08-02 is explicit that they never merge. That decision predates the evidence that a same-diff review chain closes in 2 rounds while document chains run to 15 — it is worth revisiting as a two-axis single gate rather than a merge (see R3-4).

A seventh, already-mechanised mode. Rounds spent on structure and headings are largely gone: isComplete scores documents against REQUIRED_HEADINGS by normalised word-boundary containment and feeds canonical headings + alts into every first authoring prompt. The residue shows as the pdlc-advisory-wave-gate case where the wrong completeness-gate template was checked for 7 consecutive rounds — a dispatcher bug, not a review problem.

§3Recommendations

Three tiers, ordered by how much of the pipeline's judgment they touch. Tier 1 changes no contract and can ship first. Tier 2 changes when a round is allowed to exist, gated by config. Tier 3 changes the shape of the pipeline and is the subject of §4.

Tier 2 — Loop economics config-gated

R2-1Pin-cascade confirmation round type promoted: DEC-ERRROUTE-04
Change
A new, cheap round type. If the document's own content hash is unchanged since its last approval, the round checks only the dependency cells (pins, SHAs, anchors, line refs) rather than re-reviewing the document. Sweeps batch across all downstream documents affected by one upstream move.
Mechanism
Directly nominated by the longhorizon-product-scaffold harvest ("pin-cascade round, own-hash only, batched sweeps"). Own-hash comparison is already available — DECISIONS v2–v7 carried one identical hash across six rounds, which is exactly the detection signal.
Correctness
The substantive review already happened and its verdict stands; nothing about the document changed. What is checked is precisely what did change: the dependency cells. A non-trivial own-hash delta falls back to a full round automatically.
Saving
~15–25 full review dispatches on a cascade-heavy feature — e.g. longhorizon-daily-baseline's ~15 re-confirmation dispatches, iv-snapshot-store-postgres's PLAN v4–v7 + v10–v15.
Risk
Low — narrowing what a provably-unchanged document is reviewed for.
Status
Proposed → decided (DEC-ERRROUTE-04, cross-repo consolidation pass 2026-08-27-2); implementation still pending.
R2-2Batch upstream errata into one revision per phase-window
Change
Errata raised against one upstream document within a phase window accumulate into a single revision + single delta-confirmation, instead of routing per-raiser.
Mechanism
Extends the existing per-doc erratum bound and its item-list hygiene (normalized dedupe, per-item mint tags, multi-home split) to the whole phase window. Addresses DEC-ERRROUTE-02's mis-sized channel by widening the batch rather than the round count.
Correctness
Every item still gets minted, edited, and confirmed. Re-grounding remains full-interval per DEC-ERRROUTE-03 — batching makes full re-grounding cheaper, which is the main reason it currently gets done partially.
Saving
~3–4 dispatches per collapsed batch; on features with 4–6 erratum events, 12–20 dispatches.
Risk
Medium — a larger item list per round; mitigate with the existing per-item mint tags.
R2-3Refuse downstream approval while routed errata are live
Change
A downstream document cannot receive an approving verdict while an erratum routed against its upstream is open.
Mechanism
Closes the route-never-close loop directly (DEC-ERRROUTE-01). The multi-layer propagation order already exists in the erratum protocol — "a child confirmed before its parent's decision reaches it is approved stale" — this makes that statement enforceable rather than advisory.
Correctness
Strictly tightening. It removes the stale-approval state that currently causes the re-open that causes the extra rounds.
Saving
Indirect but large: stale approvals are the entry point for the D1 cascade.
Risk
Medium — can serialize phases that currently overlap; needs a live-erratum timeout to avoid deadlock.
R2-4DEC-TERM-01 derivative stop rule
Change
A document is converged when no new ≥Medium finding has been raised for 2 consecutive rounds — regardless of remaining budget. Convergence is a derivative signal, not a verdict-at-cap.
Mechanism
Already promoted as DEC-TERM-01 in docs/_decisions/DECISIONS-loop-termination.md, nominated independently by pdlc-wave-resume (O-5) and pdlc-engineering-loop (P-2).
Correctness
The current terminal state is worse: hitting 15 accepts the document as-is with no approval at all. A derivative stop produces an approved document with a stated reason. The High-only blocking bar is unchanged, so nothing that blocks today stops blocking.
Saving
~6–9 rounds per capped document. Applied to pdlc-engineering-loop's four capped documents: roughly 24–36 rounds, 50–100 dispatches.
Risk
Medium — a genuinely slow-burning defect surfacing at round 8 would be missed. Mitigate: DoD and the V-wave still see the shipped diff.
R2-5Mechanical 5g split-halt
Change
When one High finding blocks the same document for 2 consecutive rounds, halt mechanically with a split recommendation instead of relying on a reviewer to call it.
Mechanism
On regime-swing-confirmation, 5g fired at Phase R round 3 — and nobody split. In-place revisions continued to the 15-round ceiling on six documents and 188 cross-reviews. The recurrence condition is mechanically detectable from the finding id across rounds.
Correctness
Halting is the conservative action; the operator decides. It converts an ignorable advisory into a stop.
Saving
On the worst observed feature, most of the 188-file corpus.
Risk
Medium — adds an operator stop; belongs to escalations (surface item 3), not a new burden class.
R2-6REQ ≥90% byte-ceiling relocation trigger
Change
A REQ crossing 90% of the 61,440-byte ceiling triggers a mechanical relocation pass into docs/_constraints/ before the next review round.
Mechanism
hooks/scripts/check-req-size.sh already computes the number and warns; this promotes the warning to an action. regime-swing-confirmation hit 92% at round 2 and ~90% of the content was eventually relocated anyway — after being reviewed repeatedly in the wrong document.
Correctness
Relocation moves material to a location the pipeline already reads (project-level constraints); nothing is deleted.
Saving
Large on oversized REQs — the pdlc-rcv-budget-stop (61 KB / 99.8%) and pdlc-review-convergence (311 KB) cases.
Risk
Low
R2-7Fail-closed Scope gate
Change
A cross-review missing its Scope: field fails closed rather than warning.
Mechanism
The under-tagging constraint was re-derived in 5 of 8 harvests on the regime-ledger side and was suppressed as a learning (DC-13) while remaining an un-promoted mechanism proposal row. One Scope column serving two purposes caused 2–3 halts on pdlc-learnings-injection. hooks/scripts/check-scope-field.sh already detects it interactively.
Correctness
Scope tagging is what makes cross-feature findings harvestable; an untagged finding is lost at consolidation time.

Indirect: raises harvest yield, lowers re-derivation across features.

Tier 3 — Process redesign experimental

These adapt mattpocock's skill mechanisms to an agent-vs-agent setting. The design argument is in §4; the summary rows are here.

R3-1Front-loaded grilling phase (Phase G)
Change
A bounded phase between R and F: reviewers grill the approved REQ using grilling's frontier discipline. Every resolved question becomes a decision-ledger row.
Mechanism
Rounds of numbered questions whose prerequisites are settled; dependent questions deferred; a recommended answer attached to each. Question budget ≤3 rounds. Unanswerable ⇒ escalation row, never a loop. See §4(a)–(b).
Correctness
All existing gates stay. Discovery moves earlier; it does not disappear.
Saving
+3–8 dispatches once; removes the discovery currently spread across FSPEC rounds 2–15.
Risk
High — new phase, new failure modes. One-feature experiment only.
R3-2Wayfinder-style decisions-so-far index
Change
A one-line-per-closed-decision index injected into every review dispatch, plus a never-re-litigate rule: a reviewer may re-open a closed decision only with a High finding citing new evidence and naming the decision.
Mechanism
Reuses the learnings-injection budget machinery. Dedupe key = decision id. See §4(c).
Correctness
Re-opening remains possible; it acquires a cost and a citation requirement.
Saving
Kills the re-litigation class and the D4 re-filing class; compounds across every phase.
Risk
Medium — an index that goes stale is worse than none; derive it, don't author it.
R3-3Size-tiered pipelines (S / M / L)
Change
S: merge TSPEC+PLAN into one document; skip DECISIONS and PROPERTIES. M: skip DECISIONS unless a trigger fires. L: unchanged.
Mechanism
Classification at REQ approval, from REQ size, file-ownership breadth and dependency count. Recorded in the REQ frontmatter so the choice is auditable and overridable.
Correctness
PROPERTIES on an S feature is the phase most likely to produce absence-only oracles; DoD's mutation floor and the V-wave still gate the diff. The tier is a floor, not a ceiling — a reviewer can escalate the tier.
Saving
Two of six review windows removed on S features. pdlc-rcv-budget-stop — one constant, four pure functions — is the archetype.
Risk
Medium — misclassification. Mitigate: default M, allow one-way escalation only.
R3-4Collapse CR into DoD as a two-axis review
Change
One post-implementation gate running two parallel, non-merging axes over the same diff: Standards (repo conventions + smell baseline) and Spec (does the diff match what the REQ/FSPEC asked for), with the existing wired-caller / stub / mock-data sweep retained on the Standards axis.
Mechanism
mattpocock's code-review shape: two parallel sub-agents, reports aggregated verbatim under separate headings, never merged or re-ranked, one summary per axis, no overall winner. Map pm-review onto Spec and te-review onto Standards.
Correctness
This is not the merge the 2026-08-02 decision forbids — it keeps two independent evaluators and two independent verdicts; it removes one dispatch chain and one artifact set, not one lens. Note the corroborating DEC-ORACLE-06 signal: byte-identical text across two lenses is a shared-generator signature, not independent agreement, so lens independence must be structurally enforced regardless.
Saving
~2–4 dispatches/feature plus one artifact chain.
Risk
High — contradicts a standing operator decision; needs an explicit re-decision, not a config flag.
R3-5Domain modeling → repo CONTEXT.md glossary
Change
A per-repo glossary of domain terms, injected into authoring and review dispatches, maintained as decisions land.
Mechanism
mattpocock's tdd and to-spec both consume a project CONTEXT.md / domain glossary as a first-class input. The terminology-findings class — reviewers disputing what a term means round after round — has no home today.
Correctness
Purely additive grounding; a glossary entry is a decision like any other and is subject to the never-re-litigate rule.
Saving
Removes a recurring finding class; magnitude unmeasured.
Risk
Low

§4Deep dive: grilling and wayfinder, aimed at the agents

mattpocock's grilling skill exists to extract decisions from a human. The pdlc pipeline has no human in the loop — it is /loop-driven and runs unattended. Adopting grilling verbatim is therefore impossible: a grilling agent that answers its own questions has broken the skill. The interesting question is whether the mechanism survives the substitution of authority, and the answer is yes, provided the substitution is explicit and fail-closed.

(a) The HITL problem and the authority ladder

In the original, the user is the authority: the agent asks, the user decides, and the decision is grounded in something the agent cannot fabricate. Remove the user and the frontier collapses into invention — the grillee answers from plausibility, and the resulting decisions are indistinguishable from hallucination with a paper trail.

The substitute is an authority ladder for the grillee (in the first instance, pm-author defending the approved REQ). It is ordered and exhaustive:

  1. The REQ text itself. If the REQ already answers the question, quote it. Round closed.
  2. docs/_constraints/DOMAIN-CONSTRAINTS.md and docs/_decisions/*. Project-level decided material. Quote the decision id.
  3. The code. Verified, with a file:line citation. This is the same grounding rule the phase dispatch manifests already carry — verify claims against code, cite file:line.
  4. Nothing else.

Rung 4 is the load-bearing one. A question that can only be answered by invention becomes an escalation row in docs/_queue/ESCALATIONS.md and the frontier moves on without it. This is not a new operator burden class: the documented steady-state operator surface has exactly four items and "resolving open escalations" is already item 3 (pdlc/OPERATIONS.md, §Operator surface). The resolution command already exists — pdlc decide --entry <entryId> --outcome <resolved|rejected> --by <who> — and the loop's rendered operator view already prints an id per open item.

Why this is stronger than the status quo, not weaker. Today, a question the pipeline cannot answer does not become an escalation. It becomes a finding, then a revision, then another finding — the D1/D2 loop. The ladder converts an unanswerable question from an unbounded round generator into a single bounded row with an operator on the other end.

(b) Where it slots: Phase G, between R and F

Phase G runs after REQ approval and before FSPEC authoring. Reviewers (pm-review and te-review as grillers) interrogate the approved REQ using grilling's discipline unchanged in structure:

  • Build the design tree; each decision branches into the decisions that hang off it.
  • Work in rounds. The frontier is every decision whose prerequisites are settled — ask only those now.
  • Number every frontier question and attach a recommended answer.
  • Questions whose answers depend on other open questions belong to a later round, not this one. This is the property that makes the phase terminate.
  • Fact-finding is delegated, never asked of the authority: an exploration sub-agent resolves environmental facts (rung 3) without blocking unrelated frontier questions.

Every resolved question becomes a row in the feature's decision ledger with its answer, its authority rung, and its citation. FSPEC is then authored to-spec style: pure synthesis of already-settled material, no interview, decisions pre-made, existing seams preferred over new ones.

Termination is structural, three ways. The frontier empties (grilling's own completion criterion, minus the user-confirmation step). The question budget is capped at ≤3 rounds. An unanswerable question escalates rather than recirculating. None of these depends on an agent judging that it is finished — which is exactly the judgment the corpus shows agents do not make (D2).

Expected effect. FSPEC review approaches round-1 approval, because the discovery that currently happens across review rounds 2..15 has happened up front in a forum whose structure guarantees termination. The failure mode this attacks is visible in the numbers: pdlc-engineering-loop's FSPEC ran to the 15-round cap, and pdlc-learnings-injection's did too — while both features' DoD phases, which start from a fully-decided target, closed in 2–5.

(c) What wayfinder actually contributes

The obvious reading of wayfinder — build a map of the issue tracker — is already covered here: docs/_queue/QUEUE.md is the map, dependency-ordered, and the queue drift gate and halt-row semantics are already specified. Adopting the map would be duplication.

The part worth taking is the decisions-so-far index plus the never-re-litigate rule. Concretely:

  • Every review dispatch carries a one-line-per-closed-decision index — id, one-line statement, phase closed, citation.
  • A reviewer may re-open a closed decision only with a High finding that cites new evidence and names the decision id.
  • The dedupe key for both re-litigation and staleness re-filing is the same: the decision/finding id (this is R1-4 and R3-2 sharing one mechanism).

This is the completion of an arc the pipeline is already halfway along. What already half-exists: DEC-ERR-01's absorb-don't-route rule states that routing a question the upstream has already decided is not a demoted finding but a false statement in a hand-off section; the round-2+ delta-scoped prompts already address the optimizer as the continuing author and instruct that settled decisions are not re-litigated. Both are prose instructions to an agent. Neither is enforceable, and the corpus shows what happens: 114 approving verdicts and a document still at the cap. The ledger makes the instruction a lookup with an id, which is the difference between a norm and a gate.

(d) Convergence versus correctness

The trade is explicit and it is favourable in one direction only if the arithmetic holds. Grilling costs +3–8 dispatches, once, per feature. Cascade and re-litigation rounds cost tens of dispatches, repeatedly: ~15 re-confirmation dispatches on one document of longhorizon-daily-baseline; PLAN v4–v7 and v10–v15 on iv-snapshot-store-postgres entirely bookkeeping; 54 re-filings of one hash on pdlc-engineering-loop.

Nothing in the gate set is relaxed. Structural completeness (isComplete over REQUIRED_HEADINGS) is untouched. Verdict grammar is untouched — R1-5 tightens it. Approval anchors are untouched — R1-1 makes them harder to falsify. The erratum protocol's fail-closed rules (untagged High findings fail closed; R4 halts to a POSTMORTEM) are untouched. The DoD mutation floor is untouched. What changes is when discovery happens and whether a settled question can be asked again.

(e) What not to adopt

HITL grilling verbatim Adapt, don't adopt "Wait for the user's answers" and "do not act until confirmed" have no referent here. Keep the frontier, the numbering, the recommended answers and the deferral rule; replace the authority with the ladder in (a).

Grilling adapted: the Phase G dispatch contract in full

Inputs. Approved REQ at HEAD; docs/_constraints/*; docs/_decisions/*; the decisions-so-far index; repo CONTEXT.md glossary when present.

Per round. The griller emits numbered questions, each carrying (i) the decision it hangs off, (ii) a recommended answer, (iii) the authority rung the answer is expected from. Questions whose prerequisites are unsettled are explicitly listed as deferred, with the blocking question's number.

Grillee response. For each question: answer + rung + citation (REQ section, decision id, or file:line), or ESCALATE. No answer without a citation is admissible; an uncited answer is treated as ESCALATE, which is the fail-closed direction.

Round termination. Frontier empty, or round 3 reached. Remaining deferred questions at round 3 are carried as ledger rows marked open, which makes them visible to FSPEC review rather than silently dropped.

Outputs. One decision-ledger file per feature; zero or more rows appended to docs/_queue/ESCALATIONS.md; no edits to the REQ (a REQ defect found during grilling routes through the existing erratum channel, unchanged).

Cost bound. 2 dispatches/round (griller + grillee) × ≤3 rounds, plus up to 2 exploration sub-agent dispatches for rung-3 facts = ≤8.

Measurement plan

The pipeline already emits most of what is needed, so the baseline is a query, not new instrumentation:

  • Per-dispatch payload size is already reported through report.learningsInjection.
  • Erratum events and halts are countable from POSTMORTEM files and the erratum changelog rows.

Protocol: capture these three series for the last 3 completed features as the pre-change baseline, ship Tier 1, then compare the next 3 features. Tier 2 and Tier 3 each get their own 3-feature window, one tier at a time — running them together makes the result uninterpretable. §6Rollout and correctness safeguards

Gating

Everything in Tiers 2 and 3 ships the way the advisory tier shipped: config-gated, off by default. Concretely, in .claude/pdlc.config.json: grilling.enabled: false, sizeTiers.enabled: false, cascadeConfirm.enabled: false, derivativeStop.enabled: false. Tier 1 ships ungated because every item in it is a defect fix or a tightening of an existing gate.

Experiment discipline

  • One feature per experiment. A tier enabled on a queue-wide run produces a result nothing can attribute.
  • Fail-closed gates stay fail-closed. Untagged High findings, erratum R4 halts, the completeness probe, the DoD mutation floor, the wave gate, the document oracles — none of these are in scope for relaxation, and no recommendation here touches them.
  • Fail-open where the change is an optimization. The decisions-so-far index, like the learnings injection it borrows its budget machinery from, must fail open on every error path: a missing or unreadable index degrades to today's behaviour with a notice.
  • Every new halt is an escalation row, not a new operator class. R2-5's split-halt and Phase G's unanswerable questions both land on docs/_queue/ESCALATIONS.md. The four-item operator surface is a contract — a stop condition outside it is a defect in the feature, per pdlc/OPERATIONS.md.

The skill-prompt path

Tier 3 changes reviewer and author behaviour, which means editing SKILL.md files. Those edits do not go in through a feature branch quietly: skill-prompt changes go through a CONSOLIDATION-PROPOSAL with human review, per the consolidation contract. Note also the manifest coupling — a SKILL.md edit reds the digest manifest until the digest is re-recorded, so a Tier 3 wave must budget for that step.

A3 — Dispatch cost model and prompt anatomy

Budgets. MAX_REVIEW_ROUNDS = 5 per invocation; MAX_LIFETIME_ROUNDS = 15 per document type (DEC-ROUNDS-02, operator decision 2026-08-10). A round-1 PASS phase costs 2 dispatches. Best case end-to-end before Phase I fan-out: 22–24 — R 2, F 3, T+D 3–7, P 3, PR 3, PT 1, CR 2, DOD 1, H 1, PUB 1–2, MERGE 0. Extra round: +2–3. Erratum batch: +3–4.

Prompt composition, per dispatch. Per-docType grounding clause (1–3 lines) · ORACLE_QUALITY_CLAUSE · ERRATUM_PROTOCOL_CLAUSE · PACING_CONTRACT_CLAUSE (fixed boilerplate, every round) · resume opener embedding the entire document · learningsBlock ≤20 KB (5 documents × 6 KB). Dominant costs, in order: the full-document opener on resume, the 20 KB learnings suffix, the fixed clause boilerplate.

Redundancy already removed. Delta-scoped rounds 2+ exist (REVIEW_CONVERGENCE_CLAUSE with a git-diff instruction and a High-only approval bar). Remaining redundancy is structural: CR and DoD are two dispatch chains over the shipped diff with adjacent lenses and no shared state; PT exists in two mutually-exclusive runtime paths (legacy worktree, wave V-wave); the land-proof retry is deliberate redundancy that catches a confirmer PASS without token landing.

Existing knobs, unchanged by this proposal. PHASE_DOD_ENABLED, PHASE_H_ENABLED, MODEL_DEFAULT="opus", MODEL_IMPLEMENTATION="sonnet", MODEL_QUEUE="sonnet", the round caps, mergeMode, the learningsInjection budgets, forcePhases.

SKILL.md composition. Reviewer and author SKILL files run 279–331 lines, of which ~45–55% is shared procedural boilerplate (Git Workflow, Review Process, Delta Re-Review, verdict contract). Role judgment is concentrated in one "Review Scope" block per file — which is why prompt-level trimming has low headroom and round-level changes have high headroom.
