## Positive Observations

- The one-defect-class treatment of TE F-01 + SE F-11 is the right call and is executed the right way: **one** normative rule with the scan sites enumerated in it, and four call sites that *reference* rather than restate. §1.2's own justification for that shape ("the alternative is one exclusion clause per scan site and a later reviewer having to check them against each other") is precisely why F-01 above is a one-line deletion rather than three new clauses.
- §16.3's fence paragraph does the thing that is easy to skip: it goes back to the *justification* row ("its presence means the reviewer got to the end") and states that without the exclusion that justification is false. A fix that repairs the reasoning as well as the rule is auditable at the next review; a fix that only repairs the rule is not.
- AT-64 is a genuine oracle. Deriving the seam set from `main()` rather than from a list is what makes "adding a seam without wiring it fails" an assertion instead of an aspiration, and the stated mutation ("delete one entry from `rtDevInjections`") is executable as written. It is also correctly marked as the one test in §19 that breaks the section's own pattern, so an implementer cannot quietly normalise it back to injected seams.
- AT-19's withdrawal of "this AT is the existing test extended" is worth more than the assertion it corrects. "Stating it as 'already covered' is how a gap survives a review" is the process lesson, and it was written into the document rather than into a review file that gets harvested away.
- Two of the four fixes are net deletions, and the +5.6% overrun against a +4% target is stated with its cause rather than absorbed by dropping a fix. That is the honest failure mode for a size budget.
