| F-01 | High | Local | **Mode is re-selected from the pre-dispatch measurement on *every* dispatch, so a revision episode whose dispatch is killed mid-edit flips to greenfield on the next dispatch, drops the feedback-addressing prompt for AC-3.3's resume prompt, and then reports terminal success on a round whose findings were never addressed.** AC-3.5 fixes mode selection as "selected by the pre-dispatch measurement and by nothing else": revision mode iff the artifact is present and already structurally complete. A revision dispatch legitimately *breaks* structural completeness while it works — v1.3 itself added three new top-level items (AC-3.5f, §4a A-9, R-9), and a dispatch killed after laying down a new heading but before filling it leaves the artifact structurally **in**complete on disk. The next dispatch in the same episode therefore measures "present, not complete" ⇒ **greenfield**, and AC-3.5b's re-dispatch rule then hands it "the resume prompt of AC-3.3 in greenfield mode" — a prompt that names the first unwritten section and carries **no cross-review findings at all**. The agent fills the empty section, the artifact becomes structurally complete, AC-3.5b's greenfield terminal condition (structural completeness alone, with no requirement that the dispatch returned normally or that the round's feedback was addressed) fires, and the wrapper **stops and reports success**. The round's findings are silently unaddressed, and the same convergence loop this REQ exists to harden burns a round discovering it. This is not a hypothetical population: a killed authoring dispatch *is* §H-3, the motivating incident. A test writer cannot write O-19(b)'s mandated mode-selection oracle for this case because the specified behavior is wrong rather than merely unstated, and the two candidate fixes give opposite terminal statuses. Fix at requirements altitude: make mode **sticky per episode** (an episode entered in revision mode stays in revision mode regardless of intermediate completeness), or state that a re-dispatch always carries the prompt the episode was entered with and that a revision episode's terminal condition additionally requires the dispatched revision to have completed — not structural completeness alone. | AC-3.5 scope + mode table, AC-3.5b (all three bullets), AC-3.3, AC-3.5e, O-19(b) |

## Questions

| ID | Question |
|----|---------|
| Q-01 | AC-3.5's pre-dispatch measurement is taken from the artifact "on disk". Per AC-3.2a the author commits each section, but is the measurement over the **working tree** or over the **committed** state? They diverge on the path AC-3.5e covers: a dispatch killed after a write but before its commit leaves progress in the working tree that a committed-state measurement scores as no progress (and that a later `git` operation could discard). O-13's kill seam and O-19's counter oracles both need this fixed before a fixture can be written. |
| Q-02 | AC-3.5a limb 3 compares "the artifact's bytes". When a single dispatch legitimately writes **two** artifacts (e.g. `se-author` producing TSPEC and a conditional DECISIONS), is progress evaluated per artifact — one episode each, each with its own counters — or once over the set? The AC-3.5c scope says "one artifact", which implies the former, but then a dispatch that advances only one of the two scores no progress for the other and can exhaust that artifact's consecutive budget while working correctly. |

## Positive Observations

- All five v3 findings were fixed at the altitude requested, and the two that required *retracting* a claim (the Owner row's "asserted by the wrapper's review checklist"; O-19's "how the pacing bound is checked in review") were retracted in place with the reason recorded, not quietly reworded. §4a A-9 is a genuine measurement of the seams rather than a restatement of C-1.
- The `MAX_AUTHORING_DISPATCHES` re-scope is the strongest single change in v1.3: it names both defects of the old scope, shows the whole-phase product explicitly, states the reset scope in the same breath (answering two reviewers' questions), corrects arithmetic that did not divide, and adds a Revision rule requiring consistency with `MAX_ROUNDS` to be re-shown if the scope moves again. That last clause is the part that survives this feature.
- R-9 accepts limb 3's weakness openly and shows the compensating bound, instead of arguing the predicate is stronger than it is. AC-3.1a's **Enforceability** section does the same for an unverifiable AC. A REQ that distinguishes "measurable control" from "agent-directed obligation" in its own text is directly transcribable into a TSPEC.
- AC-3.5f is a correct and non-obvious classification: it identifies that writing a POSTMORTEM for a mechanical failure would make AC-2.4's human act the exclusive exit (via AC-4.6a) and lock the pipeline into human-only recovery, and it extends AC-2.1's commit obligation so halt durability no longer depends on a POSTMORTEM existing.
- AC-4.2b's tier-1-is-incomplete clause ("mixed provenance is never used to assemble an approval") and AC-4.2c's inverted falsifying test (a record-less LEARNINGS must **pass** the guard and then fail closed at AC-4.2a) are both stated as testable obligations with the direction of failure named.

## Recommendation

**Needs revision**

Exactly what must change:

1. **F-01** — stop mode from flipping mid-episode, or state that a re-dispatch carries the prompt the episode was entered with and that a revision episode's terminal condition requires the dispatched revision to have completed. As written, a killed revision dispatch drops the round's feedback and reports success.
2. **F-02** — supply (or explicitly delegate) a structural-completeness criterion for the `CROSS-REVIEW-*` artifacts the new scope clause wraps, or exclude review dispatches from the wrapper.
3. **F-03** — anchor the tier-2 approval hash to the **approving** commit rather than to the document as it stands at harvest, so AC-4.4 can see a post-approval, pre-harvest edit.
4. **F-04** — name the wrapped population concretely, and either name the artifact measured for a code-writing remediation dispatch or exclude it and cite the bound that covers it.

F-05 and F-06 are one clause each and can ride along with F-01 and F-03 respectively. Everything else added at v1.3 — AC-2.1's extension, AC-2.3b worked example B, AC-3.1a's replace-shape correction and Enforceability section, AC-3.5c's reset scope and consistency derivation, the `MAX_AUTHORING_DISPATCHES` declaration, AC-4.2c's guard clause, AC-4.4's per-tier referent, §4a A-9, R-9, and the O-7/O-8/O-14/O-19/O-20/O-21 extensions — is approved from a testing lens.
