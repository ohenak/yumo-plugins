## AT-13a as an oracle for G-INV

**It is a real oracle, and it can fail on both regression directions.** I checked it against the two historical regressions rather than against its own description.

- **v1.0's direction (gate ahead of step 1).** Under that implementation FSPEC §12.4's worked example A halts instead of skipping. AT-13a's last conjunct — "the `FRESH` exit does **not** refuse, but names the POSTMORTEM in its skip notice", with example A driven verbatim as the fixture — is false, so the test reds.
- **v1.1's direction (gate reachable only from step 4's `STALE`/`UNEVALUABLE`).** Under that implementation the `candidate < 1` and `NOT APPROVING` exits reach `reviewLoop` without the gate. AT-13a asserts a refusal on **each** of the four run-exits independently, with AC-2.3b's example B as the `candidate < 1` fixture, so both of those conjuncts red.

Two properties make it an oracle rather than an intention. First, it is **per-exit rather than per-outcome**: an assertion that "an unresolved POSTMORTEM refuses" would pass on either regression by reaching the refusal through the one surviving path — this is the precedence-chain false-green shape, and driving each exit's fixture separately defeats it. Second, the refusal conjunct is **positive on three counts** — the phase is refused, the halt reason reproduces the Recommendation excerpt, and (§6.2 row 13) `postmortemStatus` is `"unresolved"` — not an absence-only `outcome != "ok"`. And the two fixtures are the two documents that were each unreachable under one of the two regressions, so the test's inputs are the regressions' own witnesses.

Two things it does not cover, neither of which I am filing. Its exit enumeration is closed at today's four, while G-INV is stated over "any exit added later" — the totality claim in AT-13a's name is over the exits, not over the property, and no test can be total over future exits; G-INV's "single point all such exits converge on" phrasing is the structural guard that carries that half. And §8.3 places it in `haltAndQueue.test.js` without stating the level, so whether it drives the real phase gate or a lifted helper is an implementation choice — the phase gate is not a named exported symbol in §3, so an implementer will have to drive `main()`, which is the production path and the right answer, but the row does not say so.

## Positive Observations

- **Both Highs were fixed by moving from a step number to an invariant, which is the correct generalisation of R-5.** G-INV ("step 5 is reachable only through step G … never by enumerating which steps happen to reach it today") and S-INV ("read inside `reviewLoop`; never a snapshot taken before the loop") are both stated so that the *next* added exit or added episode kind is covered without an edit. A gate that has regressed twice in opposite directions is exactly the case where the enumeration is the defect, and both fixes name that.
- **The counterfactual is stated beside each invariant.** §4.5's "a `roundIndex` derived once at phase entry … the whole phase would then be bounded by 6, not 36" and §2.5's "a gate that sits ahead of step 1 breaks A; a gate reachable only from step 4 breaks B" are the two sentences a reviewer needs to check the fix rather than trust it, and they are the two sentences most revisions leave out.
- **Three new ATs, each written in the falsifying direction.** AT-01a "reds on the 'forced skips steps 2–4' reading", AT-43a "reds on any implementation that decides mode from a pre-loop snapshot", AT-13a's five exits. Writing the AT as *what it reds on* rather than *what it checks* is what made all three verifiable in this review without reading an implementation.
- **§9.2 was updated, not just §8.3.** The H-1, H-2 and H-3 rows now cite the new ATs beside the old ones, so the defect→oracle trace does not silently keep pointing at the tests that were insufficient. My v2 N-04 complained about exactly this for H-1; it was fixed for all three.
