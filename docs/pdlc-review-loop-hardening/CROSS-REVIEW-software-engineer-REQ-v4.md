| F-01 | Medium | Local | **The approval hash's *definition* and its *derivation* name different bytes, and the difference is exactly the fail-open case AC-4.2b says it excludes.** AC-4.2b defines the field as "the content hash of the reviewed document **as it stood at that approving round**". The derivation two paragraphs later says "harvest computes the hash from **the document it is harvesting beside**", and O-21 repeats it as "harvest must compute the hash from **the document on disk**". Harvest runs at Phase H — after Phase R/F/T/P approvals, after the Final Codebase Review and after Phase DOD's remediation rounds. So the recorded value is the document's bytes *at harvest time*, not at approval. Whenever any edit lands between an approving round and harvest (a DOD remediation touching a spec, an operator fixup, a later phase's upstream-altitude finding), the record certifies **unreviewed bytes as approved**; AC-4.4's tier-2 comparison then finds HEAD equal to the recorded hash and reports "not stale", and the phase is skipped over an edit no reviewer saw. That is precisely R-1's laundering outcome and the fail-open branch AC-4.2b's own paragraph claims to have closed — the anchor is present but anchored to the wrong instant, so "the sufficiency claim is now met rather than asserted" does not yet hold. It is also self-blocking: the sha that *would* make approving-round bytes recoverable (`git show {sha}:{path}`) is demoted in the same paragraph to "**corroborating context only**", so O-21 is not obliged to make it resolvable, and after a squash merge it is not resolvable at all. Fix at this altitude, pick one: (a) the hash is computed over the document **as of the recorded approving commit**, which promotes the sha to load-bearing and requires it to be resolvable at read time on the branch harvest runs on; (b) the hash is captured **at the approving round** by whatever writes the tier-1 verdict and merely *copied* by harvest — the cleanest option, since it needs no history walk; or (c) harvest records the harvest-time hash but must also assert it equals the approving-round bytes and fail closed (record no approval) when it cannot, making the weaker record safe rather than silently wrong. | AC-4.2b (record columns + derivation paragraph), AC-4.4 tier-2 clause, O-21, R-1 |

## Recommendation

**Needs revision**

Must change before Phase F:

1. **F-01 (Medium)** — hash the document **as of the approving round** (recorded at approval, or read at the recorded approving commit), or make the harvest-time hash safe by asserting equality and failing closed. As written, tier 2 certifies harvest-time bytes as approved and fails open over any post-approval edit — the outcome AC-4.2b's own paragraph rules out.
2. **F-02 (Medium)** — reconcile AC-3.5f's "re-invoke" rationale with the `halted` row AC-2.1 makes it commit: `orchestrate-queue` excludes `halted` from pickup (`:80`, `:387`), so the queue goes idle until a human edits it. State the reset act, or drop the rationale.
3. **F-03 (Medium)** — give revision mode a continuation contract for AC-3.5b's progress-not-terminal branch, or remove that branch in revision mode. Today it re-dispatches an un-scoped feedback prompt at a partially edited document.

F-04 and F-05 are one-clause corrections and would not gate a round on their own.

None of the three blocking findings is an "AC has no oracle/fixture/seam" finding: F-01 is an internal contradiction between a normative column definition and its normative derivation, F-02 is a rationale falsified by the queue driver at HEAD, F-03 is an unspecified branch on the pipeline's new retry path. All three land in text introduced at v1.3, and §8's stopping rule answers none of them.
