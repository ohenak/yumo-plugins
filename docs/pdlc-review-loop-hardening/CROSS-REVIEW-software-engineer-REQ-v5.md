## Positive Observations

- AC-4.2d is the right fix arrived at for the right reason, and it is *smaller* than the alternatives: making tier 1 → tier 2 a copy removes the history walk, removes the squash-merge dependency, and makes the two tiers unable to disagree — three problems retired by one relocation. O-14's instruction that "a test that passes only because the hash was recomputed at harvest is the falsifying case … and must fail" is the correct way to write that oracle, because it names the passing-for-the-wrong-reason failure rather than the behaviour.
- Scope (a)'s population table is the strongest structural change in v1.4: every row that is *not* wrapped names the bound that covers it instead, the exclusion of code-writing dispatches is argued from the predicates rather than asserted, the residue is bound to **D-RLH-05** with a named successor row, and **R-11** prices the exposure honestly (including why it is smaller than §H-3's — many small files rather than one monolithic write). "Being bound by AC-3.6 therefore does not imply being wrapped by AC-3.5" is the sentence that makes the whole section unambiguous.
- AC-3.5g and R-10 together do the thing this document does well: they choose the lesser of two failures, say which failure they are accepting, and say why the rejected alternative is strictly worse ("re-issuing the round's original prompt unchanged **guarantees** re-application … rather than risking it"). Making the obligation script-decidable — assertable over the emitted prompt — while explicitly contrasting it with AC-3.1a, which is not, keeps the enforceability ledger honest.
- AC-3.5f's classification is now argued over the two things that actually differ (how many acts, and where they land), and the false premise is retracted in place with the measurement that falsified it recorded as A-10. Four rounds in, the retraction habit is what makes this REQ auditable.

## Recommendation

**Needs revision**

Must change before Phase F:

1. **F-01 (High)** — make mode a property of the dispatched prompt kind, not of the artifact's structural state, so a fresh invocation resuming a half-revised artifact cannot flip to greenfield, drop the round's findings and report success. Scope (d) closes the within-episode flip; the invocation seam that AC-2.7a's recovery path walks through is still open, and the round's cross-review files are on disk to classify it correctly.
2. **F-02 (Medium)** — name the read that actually holds the reviewed bytes (the authoring episode's post-terminal-dispatch re-measurement, or a fresh pre-review read). As written, AC-4.2d hashes either the pre-revision document or the reviewer's own file, and tier 2 then never matches HEAD, so AC-4's skip is inert.
3. **F-03 (Medium)** — measure and name the digest mechanism. A-1 measured no `crypto` and C-2 forbids the import; "the script captures it, not an agent" is not achievable by any primitive this REQ has recorded. Add A-11 plus one clause choosing an inlined pure-JS digest or an adapter seam, and state the C-5 consequence of the choice.
4. **F-04 (Medium)** — fix or drop AC-2.7a's direct-invocation route: AC-2.6a does not cover a feature that *has* a row, AC-2.1 binds the direct path, and a successful bypass leaves the row `halted`, so the queue stays idle for every other feature. AC-3.5f's "needs none" bullet must be corrected with it.

F-05 and F-06 are one-line corrections and would not gate a round on their own.

All four blocking findings land in text introduced at v1.4. None is an "AC has no oracle/fixture/seam" finding: F-01 is an invariant scoped one level too narrowly, F-02 is a normative clause naming the wrong read, F-03 is an unmeasured platform capability under the document's own DC-02 discipline, F-04 is a mis-citation whose consequence is a stranded queue. §8's stopping rule answers none of them.
